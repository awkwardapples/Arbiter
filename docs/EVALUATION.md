## Labels and splits changed (B8)

The labels and splits changed in P15, before this run.

- `expected_failing_controls`, `expected_unknown_controls` and `expected_contradiction_keys` are now produced by running the policy engine on each vendor's truth facts, the same oracle as P3-T16. Previously the failing controls were empty for all 32 vendors.
- The split is stratified per scenario code, so S06, S07, S09, S11 and S12 all have a test vendor. VND-0007 (S06+S09, a `residency.regions` contradiction) moved into the test split.

The earlier published evaluation was offline-only and had incomplete labels, so changing the splits after it is acceptable. The offline test split grew from 15 to 16 cases. Re-calibrating on the new calibration split left the gates unchanged: 0.95 for every tier, 16/16 correct, 0 unsafe approvals. Nothing under `policy/calibration/` was changed.

## Discussion

**The baseline was not measured.**
- All 10 primary baseline calls, and the 4 repeats, returned HTTP 502 from Nightshift: `nvidia/nemotron-3.5-lightning-30b-a3b_empty_response`.
- The baseline sends one large deep-lane prompt (the policy plus all the evidence). The app rule coerced it to `auto`, which routed it to Lightning, and Lightning returned an empty response even with `/no_think`.
- So the baseline's 0/10 and 10/10 INVALID measure Nightshift's routing, not single-LLM decision quality. **Do not read this run as Arbiter beating the baseline.** Its 1.000 consistency only means it was invalid every time. The baseline prompt was not changed to make it pass.
- A real comparison needs lane routing honoured. Until then, the baseline comparison is open.

**Arbiter live:**
- 10/10 correct, 0 unsafe approvals, 4/4 consistent repeats with stable fact hashes.
- Contradiction recall 1/1 (VND-0007 `residency.regions`) and failing-control recall 3/3.
- **Model calls:** 21 over the 10 primary runs (2.1 per case). 11 succeeded with Nightshift receipts:
  - plan ×6 and challenge ×1 on the deep lane;
  - extract ×2 and critique ×2 on the fast lane.
- **Models:** 9 of the 11 successful calls were served by `nvidia/nemotron-3.5-super-vl-120b-a12b`. Lightning served two deep-lane calls successfully (a plan, `rcpt_req_eu1iqmuc`, and a challenge, `rcpt_req_yaqwjyz1`); every other call routed to Lightning returned 502. Routing is intermittent, not dead.
- **Fallbacks:** 10 of 21 calls (0.476): plan ×8 (Lightning empty response) and extract ×2 (one `provider_deadline`, one bare 502). Each fell back to the deterministic step, and the trace labels it `fallback`. **6 of the 10 cases had no successful model call** and were decided on deterministic evidence-gathering. Including the 4 repeats: 25 calls, 11 succeeded, 14 fell back.
- **The fallback rate rose** from 0.20 (24 October, 5 of 25, n=5) to 0.48 (this run, 10 of 21, n=10), under the same routing rule.
- **Latency:** p50 76.3 s, p95 271.6 s per case, against p50 0.4 s offline. Successful model calls took 20–57 s each. The median is lower than on 24 October (141.8 s) largely **because** more cases lost their first model call and finished on deterministic logic; it is not a speed improvement.

Because a model only extracts, critiques, challenges and plans, and the policy engine decides, a fallback changes the evidence path but not who decides. All ten live outcomes matched their labels.

**Earlier live example (before `/no_think`).** Example 1 returned REQUEST_INFO instead of APPROVE: the model's extraction missed the vendor's MFA and encryption answers, which left C01 and C04 UNKNOWN. A worse extraction lowered the outcome; it never raised it. With `/no_think` the same example returned APPROVE in P15 (`logs/examples/live/history/trace-run-83de5da137c0.jsonl`, now archived). n is small, and this is one observation, not a rate.

**Small n.** Ten live cases give a Wilson 95% interval of 0.72–1.00 on accuracy. These numbers show that the live path works end to end. They are not a precision estimate.
