## A. Review

### Intro

Replace `REVIEW_INTRO` with this text, still inside the bordered container at the top of `page_review`:

> Use this page to record a human decision on a case the policy has flagged. Every assessment reaches a deterministic outcome through the policy engine. Review is where a qualified reviewer can override that outcome — with a rationale, and optionally an exception saved to memory for consistency with later cases.
>
> The human decision is stored and becomes part of the case record. If you tick Create precedent, the decision is also saved to H-MEM so that a later case with the same mitigations and conditions can reuse this reasoning rather than re-deciding from scratch. Tick it when you want this specific exception to be applied consistently to future cases.
>
> Only certain cases qualify for a precedent. The four requirements are checked when you click Check eligibility:
> - The mitigation fact must be TRUE in this case's data.
> - The failing control must be the one the precedent addresses (C05, current independent assurance).
> - No other control that cannot be excepted may also be failing.
> - The reviewer's role must meet the policy's authority requirement (tprm_lead for C05).

The closing sentence the brief asked for, placed at the end of that intro:

> Any case that satisfies the criteria above will be eligible. The Check eligibility button tells you whether a selected case is.

### C05 section

A second block under the intro, still above the case dropdown:

- Subheader: `About C05 (current independent assurance)`
- Body (`C05_EXPLANATION`), starting at "C05 is the control…" so the heading is not repeated:

> C05 is the control that checks whether the vendor has a current SOC 2 Type II report or ISO 27001 certificate — an independent auditor's assessment of their security controls. It is the only control where an exception is defensible, because a vendor that is mid-renewal can legitimately have a gap covered by a bridge letter. Other controls (encryption, MFA, residency, known-vulnerabilities) cannot be excepted, because they either hold or they don't.
>
> An exception (precedent) is only allowed when:
> - C05 is the specific control that is failing.
> - The vendor has a bridge letter confirming renewal within a defined window — recorded as fact assurance.bridge_letter_current = TRUE.
> - No other non-exception control is failing.
> - The reviewer has authority to grant the exception.
> The page pre-fills the mitigation key when these are satisfied.

## B. Memory

Replace `MEMORY_INTRO`. The "Try this. Create one from VND-0011…" paragraph goes away with it:

> This page shows where Precedents are stored in H-MEM memory, and the trust-ledger lines for each one. A precedent is a human exception the system has decided to remember. A case is one assessment of one vendor. The policy is the deterministic rulebook that decides outcomes. H-MEM is the memory that lets a human reviewer's past decision influence future cases — but only when the policy's exception criteria are met.
>
> Each row is one precedent: its id, the confidence the system assigns to it, the control it addresses (C05), and the ledger of every time it was created, read, or given feedback. Confidence starts at 0.70, rises with helpful feedback, and drops with wrong feedback (a wrong determination costs 0.20). Select a case below to see which of those precedents the policy considered for that case.

Above the case dropdown (both the empty and the populated branch):

- Subheader: `Precedents considered on a specific case`

Once, above the per-precedent ledger loop (only when the precedent list is non-empty):

> Ledger — the audit trail for this precedent. create is when the precedent was stored; read is when it was retrieved for a case; feedback is when a later case's outcome rated this precedent helpful or wrong.

## C. Audit

- Match (`REPLAY_MATCH_CAPTION`): `A match means the decision replays identically. The policy hash, the record hash, and every contract signature all match — nothing has changed since the decision was recorded.`
